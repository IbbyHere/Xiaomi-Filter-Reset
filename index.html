What this does

Xiaomi air purifier filters have an NFC tag that the Mi Home app reads to track "remaining life." This tool generates the password and command needed to reset that counter back to 100%, using only the filter's UID — nothing is sent over the network, everything runs locally in your browser.

Why

The tag's countdown is based on elapsed time, not actual filter condition, so the app often demands a replacement long before the filter is actually saturated. This lets you reset the counter for a filter you've inspected (and optionally cleaned) and judged still good, cutting down on unnecessary replacements and waste.

Compatible devices

Built for the Xiaomi Mi Air Purifier 4 filter. The same NFC-tag scheme is also used on other models in the line (2S, 3H, Pro, Pro H, Elite, 4 Lite, 4 Pro), so this should work for those too. This is an unofficial, community reverse-engineered method (credit to Unethical Info and Flamingo-tech) — not affiliated with or supported by Xiaomi, so results can vary by model/filter revision.

Xiaomi Filter Reset

Enter the 14-character UID read from your filter's NFC tag to generate the reset code and command.

Flashing the tag (Android + NFC Tools)

  1. Install the free NFC Tools app (by wakdev) from the Play Store on an NFC-capable Android phone.
  2. Open NFC Tools, tap Read, then hold your phone against the filter's NFC tag until it scans. Note the UID / Serial number shown (14 hex characters).
  3. Paste that UID into the field above and click Generate to get your reset command.
  4. In NFC Tools, go to Other > Advanced NFC commands, then tap accept on the warning.
  5. Set the I/O class to NfcA (ISO 14443-3A).
  6. Paste the generated reset command (the whole string, e.g. 1B...,3008,A20800000000) into the data box.
  7. Hold your phone against the filter's tag again and tap Send command.
  8. Reopen the Mi Home app — the filter should now show 100% remaining life.
Only do this for a filter you've physically checked and believe is still good — this resets the app's counter, it doesn't clean or extend the physical filter itself. Writing raw commands to an NFC tag carries a small risk of a failed/incomplete write; proceed at your own risk.
Runs entirely in your browser — no data is sent anywhere.
