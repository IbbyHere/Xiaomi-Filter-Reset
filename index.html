What this is

My Xiaomi air purifier's filter has an NFC tag in it, and the Mi Home app scans that tag to work out how much life the filter has left. Only problem is it's just counting down on a timer, it's not actually checking how dirty the filter is. So even if you've cleaned it out and it's still doing its job fine, the app will keep telling you to buy a new one.

This page spits out the code you need to reset that counter back to 100%, based on the UID printed on the filter's tag. It all runs in your browser, nothing gets sent anywhere.

What it's for

I made this for my own Xiaomi Mi Air Purifier 4, but the 2S, 3H, Pro, Pro H, Elite, 4 Lite and 4 Pro all use the same tag setup so it should work there too. None of this is official, it's built off reverse engineering done by Unethical Info and Flamingo-tech, so results might vary a bit depending on your exact filter.

Xiaomi Filter Reset

Pop in the UID from your filter's NFC tag and hit generate.

How to actually flash it (Android + NFC Tools)

  1. Grab the free NFC Tools app (by wakdev) from the Play Store. You'll need an NFC-capable Android phone.
  2. Open it up, tap Read, and hold your phone against the tag on the filter until it picks it up. Grab the UID / Serial number it shows you (14 hex characters).
  3. Paste that into the box above and hit Generate to get your reset command.
  4. Back in NFC Tools, go to Other > Advanced NFC commands and accept the warning.
  5. Set the I/O class to NfcA (ISO 14443-3A).
  6. Paste the whole reset command you just generated (e.g. 1B...,3008,A20800000000) into the data box.
  7. Hold your phone up to the tag again and tap Send command.
  8. Open Mi Home back up and the filter should now read 100%.
Only do this on a filter you've actually checked and think is still fine. This just resets what the app thinks, it doesn't clean or extend the life of the actual filter. There's also always a small chance a write fails halfway through, so do this at your own risk.
